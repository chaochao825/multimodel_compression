## Unique Bottleneck

The public released-baseline cycle is closed. Sol provides a fused speed
witness but misses the strict local quality tail; fixed SAP/EAR preserve a
stateful semantic-support mechanism, but complete public preprocessing does
not reach the joint quality/cost boundary. There is no active execution Gate.

The unique next uncertainty is a protected portfolio decision rather than an
unrun variant: reproduce an official trained sparse baseline prospectively,
test an exact fused low-precision dense path, or stop this local strict-quality
attention line. No option opens automatically. A new Gate must justify why its
function or cost class materially differs from fixed Sol/SAP/EAR and why fresh
capture or rollout has higher information value than the exposed development
nulls already recorded.

EXP-041 established that a previous denoising-step marginal-defect subspace plus
per-head ranks through 96 can represent the current defect when support and
coefficients read the current dense target. DIAG-021/022 show that the current
coefficients do not lie in a cheap causal span of old coefficients and current
sparse-output delta, even with per-rank, per-position, invariant-row, or
target-exposed per-row scaling.

Train-free temporal coefficient transport and stable-channel state prediction
are therefore closed under their registered function classes. PLAN-023 found
that current Q helps a same-identity map but does not transfer, and even the
target-exposed row-span oracle misses the quality boundary.

The remaining high-information question is no longer whether an old defect can
predict a new one or whether a larger post-hoc output map can repair it.
DIAG-024 found that the public SLA sparse branch plus affine linear-output
projection fails even its same-identity capacity ceiling. DIAG-025 then showed
that static scheduling cannot turn the current 1.512x FP8 attention kernel into
a useful strict-quality campaign: a 1.20x denoiser bound needs 91.29% coverage,
while the measured all-FP8 local error is 13.76%.

DIAG-026 found that exact algebraic coordinate changes do not remove the
tensorwise FP8 error. DIAG-027 then identified a positive mechanism: the
installed Sage per-thread smooth-K worker reached `0.9824% / 1.9204%` and
`1.589x` on the fixed step-9 cell. The pass has little robustness margin:
`s02` aggregate was `1.0116%`, while `s03` worst-head was `2.0210%`.

DIAG-028 found only `4/8` strict step-identity passes, but the failures are
concentrated: head 4 is the stable worst head across steps 7--10. A post-hoc
residual substitution of BF16 for only head 4 passes all eight cells while
retaining a `1.515x` arithmetic attention upper bound.

DIAG-029 resolved the immediate implementation-cost question positively. The
actual grouped path reached `0.8453% / 1.3543% / 0.9501%` worst observed
identity aggregate/head/tile error and `1.4931x` H200 attention speed. Its
`9.617 ms` median was only `6.27%` slower than all-head Sage and remained well
below FA3 BF16's `14.360 ms`.

PLAN-030 resolved prospective transfer more positively than coverage. The
calibration/evaluation top-2 risk heads matched in all 12 cells and error-rank
correlation was `0.958--1.000`; all 96 evaluation policy summaries passed. The
failure is therefore not an unstable risk router. Layer 0 and 29 have too much
diffuse low-precision error after their two largest-risk heads are removed, so
all eight cells fall back to BF16. Layer 14 alone supplies the four certified
cells.

DIAG-031 localized that diffuse error. Frozen labels transferred for `11/12`
cells; Layer 29 was V/PV-dominant at all steps, Layer 0 at three of four steps,
and Layer 14 remained mixed. QK-INT8/PV-FP16 is a valid diagnostic but measured
`17.070 ms`, slower than FA3 BF16 at `14.354 ms`; preserving the `9.026 ms`
FP8-PV class is mandatory.

DIAG-032 then found a narrow positive mechanism. Five percent regular
V-residual blocks were almost equivalent to correcting all blocks and to a
target-exposed sparse oracle, but passed only 4/8 cells. Raw adaptive rank-8
also failed most cells. Applying adaptive rank-8 after sparse V shaping passed
8/8 at `0.229--0.799%` aggregate, `0.301--1.161%` worst-head, and
`0.311--0.852%` worst-tile error. The unique bottleneck is now whether that
shaped tail admits a calibration-fixed or current-content-observable rank-8
output-channel basis. Exact probability mass and target-projected rank-8 remain
nondeployable, and no speed can be inferred from their arithmetic cost.

DIAG-033 separated tail capacity from basis transfer without leakage. A frozen
cell rank-8 basis passed 7/8 evaluation cells, frozen tile rank-8 passed 6/8,
and Sage-output rank-8 passed 6/8. The sole static-basis hard cell was Layer 29
step 12, where frozen cell rank-8 reached `0.965% / 1.507% / 5.139%` and even
rank-16 left `5.036%` worst-tile error, while target-exposed adaptive rank-8
reached `0.325% / 0.689% / 0.419%`. The failure concentrates in held-out `s05`,
head 9, tiles 7/0; frozen/adaptive overlap on the worst head-tile was only
`0.051`. The next bottleneck is not static rank but whether a tiny adaptation
can shape V/PV quantization error into a stable, foldable coordinate system
without adding an online tail branch.

DIAG-034 found that this foldable coordinate hypothesis fails for shared
orthogonal Cayley V rotations: fixed Hadamard and learned rank-4/rank-8
transforms changed Stage-A V-only E4M3 error energy by less than 1%, so the
registered 10% gate stopped before held-out payloads.

DIAG-035 then tested whether the numerical tail was regular-block sparse before
attention. At 5.08% density, V-risk recovered only about `5--6%` of V-error
energy. Even exact mass correction passed only `5/8` cells on the faster base,
and quality fallbacks reduced its optimistic speed envelope to about `1.40x`.

DIAG-036 finally separated operand scale error from the online P/PV floor using
a source-faithful Sage SM90 counterfactual. Tokenwise Q/K plus block-group16 V
reduced stock error energy by only `1.1--4.9%` per cell and passed `0/8`.
Block-channel V increased scale metadata roughly 256-fold over global-channel
V while improving V operand error only about 2.4% relatively. Perfect BF16 Q/K
plus block-channel E4M3 V still passed `0/8`; even the BF16-QKV control retaining
modeled online P/PV passed only `7/8`. The unique bottleneck is therefore no
longer an untested train-free operand proxy. It is a portfolio decision between
reproducing a trained sparse/quantized baseline and accepting closure of this
custom strict-quality low-precision attention line. Neither option is an active
gate, and DIAG-029's exposed implementation witness does not override
DIAG-030's prospective `33.3%` coverage boundary.

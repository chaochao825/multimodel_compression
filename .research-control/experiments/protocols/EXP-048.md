# EXP-048: Distillation-induced low-rate state-closure cross

- Status: complete
- Claim: C-027
- Candidate: L-027
- Gate: G-027
- Kind: prospective exploratory side probe

## Question

At the same low-rate state payload, do rCM weights induce a broadly more
predictable and open-loop-stable late-layer dynamics than teacher weights, or is
any apparent gain explained only by the rCM on-policy latent trajectory?

## Frozen two-by-two design

For weights `M in {teacher, rCM}` and latent trajectories
`X in {native4, rCM4}`, evaluate all four cells `D(M, X)`. Native4 uses the
unchanged teacher checkpoint with four-step UniPC and CFG 5. rCM4 uses the
official four-step stochastic rCM equations. Cross-fed calls use the trajectory's
own latent and timestep with the selected model weights and the same positive
text embedding.

The decomposition reports:

- weight effects at fixed `X`;
- trajectory effects at fixed `M`;
- the weight-by-trajectory interaction in log risk.

## Frozen scope and identities

- Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, F17 480p, token grid 5x30x52.
- Contiguous blocks 20--29 and four macro denoising stages.
- Four fresh calibration prompts/seeds and four fresh selection prompts/seeds.
- Calibration stores 256 deterministic token rows per stage. Selection evaluates
  all tokens and cannot update any fitted artifact.
- No CFG-branch claim is made: rCM is a guided conditional student, while the
  native4 trajectory itself still uses the registered teacher CFG path.

## Fixed-payload state family

For rank `r in {16,32,64}`, retain one token state `C_k in R^(N x r)` and a
calibration-only orthonormal channel basis `U in R^(D x r)`:

`C_k = r_k U`.

The primary first-order innovation transition is

`C_hat_k = a_k * C_hat_(k-1) + b_k * (h_k - h_(k-1)) U`,

with one pair of coordinatewise coefficients per layer and macro transition.
`reuse`, state AR(1), and a one-step two-lag innovation model are controls. H2/H3
rollouts use only the compressed state at the last exact anchor and current
block-input drift. Basis and coefficients are fitted on calibration only.

Two basis scopes are mandatory:

1. model-specific basis shared across both trajectories;
2. one shared basis across both models and trajectories.

The first tests existence at fixed payload; the second diagnoses whether an
apparent benefit is only a model-specific hidden gauge.

## Metrics and cost

- Residual and whole-block-output relative L2 per identity, model, trajectory,
  basis scope, layer, rank, target stage, method, and horizon.
- Calibration-basis capacity, worst-identity risk, H1/H2/H3 amplification,
  two-lag history advantage, and shared-basis penalty.
- BF16 dynamic state bytes, persistent basis bytes, and projection/render/state
  arithmetic. These are not latency measurements.

## G-027

At rank 64 on the selection split, G-027 passes only if all hold:

- rCM-on-rCM capacity reaches aggregate/worst output L2 at most 0.5%/1% in at
  least 6/10 layers;
- rCM-on-rCM H1 innovation reaches at most 1%/2% in at least the same breadth;
- rCM weights improve H1 risk by at least 25% versus teacher weights on both
  native4 and rCM4 inputs in at least 6/10 layers;
- rCM-on-rCM H2/H3 aggregate/worst errors stay at most 2%/4%;
- the two-lag H1 advantage is at most 10%, and the shared-basis H1 penalty is at
  most 25%.

If the weight-improvement breadth passes but an absolute or stability guard
fails, record `directional-only`; it does not authorize training. Otherwise the
outcome is `null/adverse`.

## Resource and stop rule

One H200 on server 236, at most eight GPU-hours and 30 GiB. A stopped foreign
process may retain memory on the selected GPU; therefore no performance claim is
permitted. Stop on one valid outcome, two integration repairs, split leakage,
capture inequivalence, incomplete/nonfinite rows, or resource exhaustion.
